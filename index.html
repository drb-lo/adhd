01 · DIAGNOSISDSM-5-TR

The criteria are a starting point

Symptoms of inattention or hyperactivity-impulsivity must

  • Reach ≥6 of 9 in either domain below (≥5 if age ≥17)
  • Be present before age 12
  • Occur in ≥2 settings — home + school. Only at home → consider ODD or a parenting mismatch. Only at school → consider an undiagnosed learning disorder
  • Interfere with or reduce academic, social, or occupational functioning
  • Be present ≥6 months and inconsistent with developmental level

Symptoms of inattention (9)

  • Fails to give close attention or makes careless mistakes
  • Difficulty sustaining attention in tasks or play
  • Doesn't seem to listen when spoken to directly
  • Doesn't follow through on instructions; fails to finish tasks
  • Difficulty organizing tasks and activities
  • Avoids or dislikes tasks requiring sustained mental effort
  • Loses things necessary for tasks or activities
  • Easily distracted by extraneous stimuli
  • Forgetful in daily activities

Symptoms of hyperactivity-impulsivity (9)

  • Fidgets with hands/feet or squirms in seat
  • Leaves seat when remaining seated is expected
  • Runs or climbs excessively (or feels restless, older patients)
  • Unable to play or engage in leisure activities quietly
  • "On the go," acting as if "driven by a motor"
  • Talks excessively
  • Blurts out an answer before a question is completed
  • Difficulty waiting their turn
  • Interrupts or intrudes on others

THREE PRESENTATIONS — INATTENTIVE (MAYA'S LANE, MOST MISSED) · HYPERACTIVE-IMPULSIVE (MOSTLY PRESCHOOLERS) · COMBINED (MOST COMMON REFERRAL) — CAN SHIFT WITH AGE

"Before age 12" and "2 environments" are not hard-and-fast rules.
DSM-5-TR · APA 2022 · AAP CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE 2019
01 · DIAGNOSISSYMPTOM DOMAINS

What you see depends on when you look

Inattention (9)

  • Careless mistakes · can't sustain attention
  • Doesn't seem to listen · doesn't finish tasks
  • Can't organize · avoids sustained mental effort
  • Loses things · easily distracted · forgetful

Hyperactivity-Impulsivity (9)

  • Fidgets · leaves seat · runs/climbs · can't play quietly
  • "Driven by a motor" · talks excessively
  • Blurts answers · can't wait turn · interrupts
Hyperactivity is what gets children referred. Inattention is what does the long-term academic damage — and it's the domain that doesn't fade. An adolescent who "grew out of ADHD" usually just grew out of the visible half.

01 · DIAGNOSISRECOGNITION BIAS

Who gets diagnosed — and who gets missed

Why girls are missed

  • Present inattentive — quiet failure, not disruption; teachers refer disruption
  • Community sex ratio ~2:1, but clinic samples historically 3–4:1 → referral bias, not biology alone
  • More internalizing comorbidity (anxiety) that absorbs the diagnostic attention
  • Median diagnosis comes years later than boys — often at an academic transition, like Maya at 12

Why age changes the picture

  • Preschool: hyperactivity dominates — but so does normal development; diagnose carefully
  • School-age: classic combined presentation, peak referral years
  • Adolescent: inattention persists, hyperactivity internalizes to restlessness — "lazy," "unmotivated," "anxious" are the mislabels
NSCH 2022 · DANIELSON ET AL, J CLIN CHILD ADOLESC PSYCHOL 2024

What the numbers mean

  • No instrument rules ADHD in or out alone — LRs of 3–4 shift, they don't decide
  • Concordant parent + teacher positives multiply → probability climbs fast
  • Discordant reports move probability little — that's your cue to widen the differential, not to average the scores

Screening caution

In low-prevalence (unselected) populations, PPV of a positive Vanderbilt is poor (~0.19 in community samples). These are case-finding tools for symptomatic children, not screeners for everyone.

01 · DIAGNOSISMIMICS & COMPANY

What else looks like ADHD — and what travels with it

Mimics — rule out

  • Sleep — OSA, restless legs, the phone under the pillow
  • Anxiety / depression — inattention as rumination
  • Learning disorder — inattention only during academics? Test.
  • Absence seizures — brief, frequent "daydreaming" spells
  • Hearing/vision · thyroid · substances (adolescents) · trauma

Comorbid — often BOTH

  • Anxiety disorders ~25%
  • Learning disorders ~30–45%
  • ODD ~30–40%
  • Depression, tics, ASD

02 · MANAGEMENTNON-MEDICATION · 1/3

Behavior therapy is THE treatment

Age 4–5 · behavioral therapy is first-line

  • PTBM before any prescription — evidence-based, durable, no side effects
  • Medication only for moderate-severe symptoms with failed PTBM and significant impairment
  • Why: preschool brains, preschool trials (PATS) — less benefit, more side effects

Age 6+ · behavioral therapy is adjunct

  • Medication is first-line for the core symptoms — that's what the MTA showed (coming up)
  • Behavior therapy adds most for oppositionality, parenting stress, skills, comorbid anxiety
  • Best results: both. Realistic clinics: med + school supports, add PTBM where available
Say it to parents plainly: "Medication treats the attention system. Behavior therapy teaches the skills. They fix different problems."
AAP CPG 2019 · WOLRAICH ET AL, PEDIATRICS 2019

02 · MANAGEMENTNON-MEDICATION · 3/3

School supports — and what to tell parents about everything else

504 Plan vs IEP

  • 504 — accommodations: extended time, preferential seating, movement breaks, reduced homework load. ADHD qualifies as a disability affecting learning
  • IEP — specialized instruction (usually under "Other Health Impairment"); needed when accommodation isn't enough or a learning disorder coexists
  • Process starts with a parent's written request to the school — your letter documenting the diagnosis is the door-opener. Write it at the diagnosis visit

Low / no evidence — answer confidently

  • Elimination / restriction diets — small effects in small subsets; not recommended routinely
  • Omega-3 supplements — statistically detectable, clinically trivial (SMD ≈ 0.1–0.2)
  • Neurofeedback — no better than sham in blinded trials
  • Sugar — repeatedly acquitted in controlled studies
  • Cognitive/working-memory training — trains the game, not the child
Exercise and sleep hygiene are the honorable mentions: real but modest benefits, and you'd recommend them anyway.
SONUGA-BARKE ET AL, AM J PSYCHIATRY 2013 (NONPHARM META-ANALYSIS) · CORTESE ET AL 2016 (NEUROFEEDBACK)
02 · MANAGEMENTLANDMARK · MTA 1999

MTA: why medication is first-line at 6+

FULL TITLE — "A 14-MONTH RANDOMIZED CLINICAL TRIAL OF TREATMENT STRATEGIES FOR ATTENTION-DEFICIT/HYPERACTIVITY DISORDER," MTA COOPERATIVE GROUP

Design

N=579, ages 7–9.9, combined-type ADHD, randomized ×14 months to: medication management (careful titration + monthly follow-up) · intensive behavioral · combined · community care.

Core results

  • Med management > behavioral alone and > community care for core symptoms
  • Combined ≈ meds alone on core symptoms — but better for comorbid anxiety, ODD, parent satisfaction, and allowed lower doses
  • Community care (mostly meds, poorly titrated!) underperformed — titration quality is the drug

Long-term follow-ups — be honest

  • Group differences fade by 36 months as treatment quality regresses to community norms
  • Growth: cumulative stimulant exposure ↔ ~1–2 cm lower adult height; weight effects front-loaded
The MTA's lesson: carefully titrated medication with scheduled follow-up beat everything else, including casually prescribed medication.
MTA COOPERATIVE GROUP, ARCH GEN PSYCHIATRY 1999 · SWANSON ET AL 2017 (GROWTH)

Direct RCT verdict

OROS-MPH statistically superior to atomoxetine. Both beat placebo. This — plus Cortese — is why stimulants are first-line and non-stimulants are for stimulant failure, intolerance, contraindication, or family preference.

Roughly 4 in 10 non-responders to one agent responded to the other. Non-response to your first drug is not non-response to the class — or to treatment.

02 · MANAGEMENTINITIATION

Starting and titrating: the MTA discipline in clinic form

Choosing the first agent

  • Class: methylphenidate-class first in most children (tolerability); amphetamine-class is reasonable, especially adolescents
  • Formulation: long-acting preferred — coverage, adherence, less stigma (no nurse's office dose)
  • Practicalities decide the rest: can they swallow pills? (sprinkles/liquids exist) · what hours need coverage? · what does insurance cover generic?

Titration rhythm

  • Start low → adjust every 1–2 weeks; contact (call/portal/visit) at each step
  • Re-collect parent AND teacher Vanderbilt follow-up forms at each step and adjust on the scores
  • Target best response, not first response — the MTA's community-care arm failed by stopping at "a bit better"
  • ~70–80% respond to the first stimulant; >90% to at least one of the two classes
Expectation-setting script: "We need your information to make the perfect regimen for you."
AAP 2019 · MTA TITRATION PROTOCOL · GREENHILL ET AL
02 · MANAGEMENTMONITORING · 1/2

Before the first script, and at every visit after

Baseline — history beats testing

  • Cardiac screen: exertional syncope/chest pain, palpitations, family hx of sudden death <35, congenital heart disease, WPW/long QT
  • Exam + baseline height, weight, BP, HR
  • No routine ECG (AAP): Cooper 2011, NEJM — 1.2M person-years, no increase in serious cardiovascular events with ADHD medication. ECG/cardiology only for positive screen
  • Screen for personal/family psychosis, mania, substance use

Ongoing cadence

  • Every visit: height, weight (plot them!), BP, HR, sleep, appetite, mood
  • Titration phase: monthly contact
  • Stable: q3 months — conveniently the Schedule II refill rhythm
  • Repeat Vanderbilts at least yearly and at every dose change
Expected physiology, not a complication: BP +2–4 mmHg, HR +3–8 bpm.
AAP 2019 · COOPER ET AL, NEJM 2011

02 · MANAGEMENTTROUBLESHOOTING · 2/2

"It's not working" — salt-switching and half-life games

The escalation ladder

  • 1 · Verify — adherence, timing, teacher data (is it "not working" or "wearing off at 2 pm"? Different problems)
  • 2 · Optimize — push dose to best response or first side effect; most "failures" are under-dosing
  • 3 · Switch the salt — MPH ↔ AMPH. ~40% of non-responders to one class respond to the other; >90% respond to at least one
  • 4 · Only then — non-stimulant, or FDA-approved augmentation (guanfacine ER + stimulant)
  • 5 · Still stuck — rethink the diagnosis and the comorbidity list, not the pharmacy

Half-life engineering — match the curve to the day

  • Wears off during homework → longer tail: Vyvanse (10–13 h), Concerta (ascending 22:78 release)
  • Rough first hour of school → faster front: Adderall XR / Ritalin LA (50:50 beads) — or Jornay PM, which is working before breakfast
  • Evening needs (sports, driving, homework) → add 5–10 mg IR at 3–4 pm rather than raising the morning dose
  • Peak-hour "zombie", good bookends → smoother curve: Vyvanse's prodrug kinetics
You are drawing a concentration-time curve over a child's schedule.
NEWCORN 2008 (CROSS-RESPONSE) · FORMULATION KINETICS: FDA LABELS
02 · MANAGEMENTSPECIAL SITUATIONS

Four situations from your first month of clinic

Comorbid anxiety (~25%)

Stimulants remain first-line — treating the ADHD often improves anxiety driven by chronic failure. If anxiety clearly worsens on stimulant: atomoxetine (treats both).

Tics / Tourette

Not a contraindication. RCT meta-analysis: no increased tic risk. If tics escalate on treatment: guanfacine ER (helps both) or atomoxetine. Alpha-2 agonists are the two-birds drug.

Adolescent diversion & misuse

Ask directly, every visit: "Has anyone asked you to share or sell your pills?" Prefer LDX (prodrug — can't snort/inject a benefit) or non-stimulants when risk is real. And the registry data: medicated periods = ~30–40% fewer crashes, injuries, arrests, and later substance-use disorder. Treatment is the anti-drug.

Cardiac red flags

Positive screen (exertional syncope, concerning FHx, known CHD, arrhythmia) → ECG ± cardiology before starting. Everyone else: no ECG. Stimulants are usually still possible after cardiology clears.

COHEN 2015 (TICS) · LICHTENSTEIN ET AL, NEJM 2012 · CHANG ET AL 2017 · QUINN ET AL 2017 (SUD)

02 · MANAGEMENTBREAKOUT · ASD & MOOD

Breakout: ADHD + autism, ADHD + mood disorder

Co-occurring ASD (30–50% of ASD has ADHD sx)

  • DSM-5 allows both diagnoses; treat the ADHD — it's often the most impairing, most treatable piece
  • Stimulants work, but expect less: RUPP methylphenidate trial in ASD — response ~49% (vs 70–80% typical), smaller effect sizes, more irritability and discontinuation
  • Rule: start lower, go slower — half the usual start, longer intervals
  • Guanfacine ER has solid dedicated evidence in ASD (Scahill 2015) — earlier on the list here than usual

Co-occurring mood symptoms

  • Irritability is ADHD's most common travel companion — it is not bipolar disorder. Episodic mood change, grandiosity, decreased need for sleep are; chronic frustration-intolerance isn't
  • Screen every adolescent for depression (PHQ-A) — untreated ADHD is a depression risk factor
  • Sequence by the biggest fire: severe depression/mania first, then ADHD. Moderate mood + clear ADHD → often treat ADHD first and reassess; academic rescue lifts mood
  • Stimulants do not destabilize treated, stable mood disorder
RUPP AUTISM NETWORK, ARCH GEN PSYCHIATRY 2005 · SCAHILL ET AL, AM J PSYCHIATRY 2015 · AACAP PRACTICE PARAMETERS
03 · RESOLUTIONMAYA · 12F

So: Focalin XR or Vyvanse?

The evidence-first answer

Start methylphenidate-class: Focalin XR 5 mg qAM, titrate by 5 mg weekly with repeat Vanderbilts, target best response ≤30 mg.

  • Cortese: MPH = best efficacy-tolerability balance in children/adolescents
  • Stimulant-naive 12-year-old → tolerability first, effect size second
  • 8–12 h covers school + homework start

When Vyvanse wins instead

  • Homework runs to 8 pm → 10–13 h coverage
  • Can't swallow pills → dissolve in water (Focalin XR sprinkles too)
  • Older sibling with misuse history in the house → prodrug advantage
  • Insurance formulary says so — both are generic now; check first
  • And it's the likely second agent if MPH-class under-delivers at optimized dose

03 · CLOSETAKE-HOMES

Five things to keep

1Priors first. Chief complaint of school failure ≈ 30–50% pre-test probability. The Vanderbilt (LR+ 3–4) refines; concordant parent + teacher forms decide; discordance widens the differential.
2The missed phenotype is an inattentive girl. Boys 13%, girls 7% diagnosed — much of that gap is referral bias, and it's diagnosed years late, at academic transitions.
3Age picks the first move. 4–5: parent training. 6+: stimulant + behavioral + school supports. Adolescent: long-acting/prodrug + the diversion talk.
4Methylphenidate-class first in children (Cortese); optimize dose, then switch salts before abandoning stimulants — ~40% cross-response. Non-stimulants have a defined second-line role.
5Titration quality is the drug. MTA's real lesson: scheduled follow-up, rating-scale data, height/weight/BP/HR every visit, no routine ECG.

B1 · BACKUPPRESCHOOL DETAIL

Backup — PATS: methylphenidate at 4–5

  • N=303, ages 3–5.5, moderate-severe ADHD, all failed 10 wk of parent training first
  • MPH IR effective vs placebo but effect sizes ~0.4–0.8 — smaller than school-age (~1.0)
  • More emotional lability, irritability, appetite/sleep effects; 11% discontinued for adverse events
  • Slower MPH clearance in preschoolers → lower mg/kg doses; optimal mean dose ~14 mg/day divided
  • Clinical translation: behavioral first is not just guideline piety — the risk-benefit genuinely shifts at this age

B2 · BACKUPSCORING HOW-TO

Backup — scoring the Vanderbilt in 60 seconds

  • Items 1–9 inattention, 10–18 hyperactivity/impulsivity; each 0–3 (never→very often)
  • A symptom "counts" if scored 2 or 3; need ≥6 counted in a domain (parent form)
  • Plus a performance impairment item scored 4–5 (or 1–2 on the reverse-scored school forms) — symptoms without impairment don't qualify
  • Follow-up versions track symptom scores + side effects during titration — use them at every dose change
  • Comorbidity screens built in: ODD/conduct items, anxiety/depression items — flag, don't diagnose
  • Free from NICHQ; scoring instructions ship with the form
